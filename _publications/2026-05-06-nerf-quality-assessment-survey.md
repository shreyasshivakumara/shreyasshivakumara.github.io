Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) have transformed photorealistic rendering by enabling the generation of novel viewpoints from sparse sets of input images, making them valuable for gaming, filmmaking, and augmented and virtual reality applications. However, evaluating the quality of NeRF-synthesized outputs remains a significant challenge. Subjective evaluations are the gold standard but are time-consuming and difficult to scale, while objective metrics often fail to reliably capture NeRF-specific distortions.

In this survey, we review the subjective evaluation methods commonly used for NeRF and analyze their limitations, then outline existing objective metrics and their strengths and weaknesses in the NeRF context. To assess their practical utility, we conduct an empirical study of 21 objective metrics, including NeRF-specific ones, across synthetic and real-world datasets.
